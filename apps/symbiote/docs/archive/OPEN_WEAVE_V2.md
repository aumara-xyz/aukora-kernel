**2. Community governance as covering space**
When you move from one person to a community, the topology shifts from knot theory to sheaf theory.

Each person is a **stalk** — a local space with their own topology, their own knots and unknottings. The community is the **base space**. Governance is the **restriction map** — the structure that says: when two people's local realities overlap, how do they agree?

This is the critical distinction from both top-down hierarchy and flat consensus:

*   **Flat consensus pretends there's no base space.** Everyone is just their own stalk, floating. That's ungoverned — it can't coordinate because there's no mechanism for local agreement to extend to global coherence.

Practically, this means: decisions in an Aumara community don't require everyone to agree (that's collapsing stalks). They require that the decision is **compatible with the sheaf condition** — that local agreement on overlapping domains extends to a global section. If two people agree on what care means in the space where their lives touch, that agreement can propagate across the community without either person having to surrender their local topology.

The power of this framing is **sheaf cohomology**. It gives you precise tools to measure where the global structure fails — where local agreement doesn't extend to global coherence.

### Thermodynamics: An Open System
The circulation metaphor needs a thermodynamics. Without one, "value circulates" is a claim, not a mechanism. Here it is.

| **Reservoir** | **What It Is** | **Role in the System** |

| **The Biosphere** | Internal circulation | Aura flow through the community — the closed loops where value returns enriched |
| **The Waste Stream** | Entropy production | Conflict, misunderstanding, extraction, hoarding — the heat that must be exported |

**The second law:** Entropy always increases globally. In a closed system, this means heat death — equilibrium, stagnation, everything the same temperature, nothing moves. But Aumara is not closed. It is open to the sun.

**The key move:** You maintain local order by exporting entropy. The community creates structure (care, coherence, governance) by dumping disorder into the waste stream. This is not a moral failure. **Extraction as entropy export without order creation.** In thermodynamic terms, extraction is a process that takes energy from the biosphere (the internal circulation) and dumps it into the waste stream without creating corresponding structure. A corporate profit is thermodynamically identical to a heat engine that extracts work from a temperature gradient and dumps waste heat into the environment. The gradient is the labor-capital asymmetry. The work is the shareholder return. The waste heat is the depleted community. The Aumara topology makes this visible: extraction shows up as a **topological sink** — a point where flow enters but doesn't leave, where the divergence of the Aura current is negative, where entropy is produced without structure.

**Regeneration as order creation that exceeds entropy production.** A regenerative act is one where the structure created (a new skill, a healed relationship, a built commons) contains more information than the entropy dissipated in its creation. Biologically: a tree that captures sunlight and builds cellulose creates more order than the heat it radiates. In Aumara: a teacher who transfers knowledge creates more coherence than the friction of the transfer. The measure is **net negentropy**: structure created minus entropy exported.

**The heat death problem.** What prevents the system from reaching equilibrium? In a closed economy, accumulation drives toward heat death — everyone ends up with the same amount, nothing moves. In Aumara, the sun prevents this. The external creative input is not a metaphor. It is the thermodynamic engine: new ideas, new people, new problems, new solutions. Without it, the circulation would slow and stop. With it, the system stays open, far from equilibrium, alive.

**The anti-hoarding mechanic, thermodynamically.** Hoarding is local accumulation that blocks flow. Thermodynamically, it is a **local equilibrium** — a pocket where the system has reached maximum entropy locally, but at the cost of global disequilibrium. The hoarder is a cold spot in a warm system, a dead zone where circulation has stopped. The topology corrects this not by punishment but by geometry: high local curvature creates gradients that drive flow around the blockage. The network routes around the stone.
